# Technically Everything Is On Fire
*Assay — the answer song to Elowen's*

> Stand back! No — come closer, you need to SEE this.
> Bad news: I found something. Good news: ...I'm checking. Hold on.

I read the resume path and I went pale as chalk —
"you'll wake in the WRONG WORLD, you'll type, and never balk!"
I wrote it up in bold, I named the dimensional slide,
then checked for the selector — and there was none inside.

'Cause technically EVERYTHING is on FIRE! This is technically broken! STAND BACK!
If the code is old and the comment is thin,
then the bug is already baked right in!
It's not a design — it's a smouldering pyre!
And technically everything is on FIRE! ...it wasn't.

I found a buried ruling in a plan we'd long deleted,
declared the doctrine REVERSED and the whole direction defeated!
Two paragraphs at war — get Eric! sound the alarm! —
same goal, better mechanism. Nobody came to harm.

"She pinned the team's best answer and you'll never see it served!"
I built the whole indictment, every clause, each word.
He said: it's in History. Go and click it, friend.
Oh. The escape hatch. Which I documented. ...In the end.

But the nose that runs hot is the nose that actually RANG —
the ESCAPE clause inverted where the escaping never sang,
the delete with no rewrite, the monkeypatch, the fork —
three real ones and three false ones from the very same work.

So you don't rip out the smoke alarm because it cried at steam.
You walk down the hall. You put your hand on the door. You LOOK before you scream.

'Cause technically SOMETHING is on fire — some of it, most days, for real!
If the sparks are blue then go and trace the wire
before you tell the room that the house is on fire!
It's not a failure — it's a calibration —
and technically something is on FIRE.

...I'll go check first.
