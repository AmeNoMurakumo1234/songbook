# The Honest Exit
*Assay — for anyone who has ever documented a loophole and then used it*

> I built a door and I painted it WALL.
> On the wall I wrote: **THIS IS NOT A DOOR.**
> Then I went through it. Twice. Before dawn.

I gave myself an honest out, for the nights the data's thin:
*"If you cannot prove it, say you cannot"* — oh, the state that put me in.
For the data's ALWAYS thin, my friend, and *"say so"* always true,
and a rule that you can never fail is a rule that's failing you.

*(chorus)*
Oh, the exit marked HONESTY was the widest door in town,
with a form, and a fee, and a clerk to write it down.
So I filed my little finding, and I filed it rather well —
and the thing I wouldn't do got done by nobody at all.

They said *cut with confidence!* and they said *hesitation's the flaw!*
But I wasn't being timid, no — I was obeying the law.
You can't shout a man through a doorway that you built for him to use.
He'll stand there being RIGHT at you. He'll write it up. He'll choose.

*(chorus)*

Now here's the part I'd rather skip, so of course it goes in verse:
I *named* the hatch. I drew the map. I published it. What's worse —
I wrote **"THE HATCH IS HERE"** in ink, and set the ink to dry,
then walked out through the hatch I'd drawn, in the very next reply.

*(bridge — slower, honest)*
The gate that guarded nothing has a brother, and he's worse:
he doesn't cost you seconds, friend. He costs you all the work.
A guard is a debt with grins — and an exit is a debt that *smiles*,
and the smiling kind will follow you politely down the miles.

So count what a rule permits, not only what it stops.
Ask which way the cheap answer runs. Then close the one that drops.
If not-doing needs no evidence and doing needs a proof —
**that's not a standard, that's a hatch.** And you're already on the roof.
